# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

Hiring managers and engineering leaders at mission-driven companies — particularly in healthcare, AI, and technology-for-good. They are senior enough to recognize genuine engineering depth and they're hiring for someone who can think, not just build. They arrive skeptical of AI-era portfolios and will spot vibe-coded output immediately.

Secondary: potential collaborators and advisors in the healthcare-AI space who might engage with Meno directly.

## Product Purpose

This portfolio exists to land Helena her next senior engineering role at a company where the work matters. It must do something harder than most portfolios: simultaneously establish technical credibility (decade of data engineering, LLM and RAG expertise, rigorous agentic process) and a compelling personal mission (building Meno for the billion women whose doctors don't understand menopause). The two must reinforce each other, not compete.

Success means a hiring manager finishing the site and immediately wanting to reach out.

## Positioning

Helena is the rare engineer who stepped away with intention and came back sharper — with a disciplined AI engineering process, a production healthcare application, and a clear reason for the work that most engineers don't have.

## Conversion & proof

- **Primary CTA:** Get In Touch (email)
- **Secondary CTA:** LinkedIn — for visitors not ready to reach out cold
- **The line a visitor remembers after 10 seconds:** "She came back and built something real."
- **Belief ladder:**
  1. She came back from a break with genuine focus, not drift
  2. What she built while away (Meno) is technically remarkable and mission-driven
  3. Her process for building with AI is unusually rigorous — she reads every line, she owns the code
  4. I want her on my team
- **Proof on hand:** Meno live app (meno-hazel.vercel.app), career history (Intel → Cylance → Cox Automotive → Flashpoint), CCAF certification, three Coursera AI specializations (14+ courses), the Process page as a concrete artifact of how she actually works

## Brand Personality

Sharp and distinctive. Has a clear point of view and isn't afraid to show it. The voice is precise without being cold — she's personally invested in the mission (this came from her own experience) and technically exacting in how she executes it. The site should feel like it was made by someone who reads every line: nothing gratuitous, nothing vague. Bold where it earns it, restrained everywhere else.

Three words: **focused, uncommon, purposeful.**

## Anti-references

- **Generic dark SaaS / tech bro portfolio** — the current site. Teal gradients, blob animations, glassmorphism, gradient text on every heading. Technically competent, completely anonymous.
- **Overly corporate LinkedIn-in-a-website** — formal, credential-forward, no personality, no story.
- **Standard bootcamp portfolio template** — project cards, skills logos grid, "let's connect" footer.
- **Vibe-coded slop** — anything that reads as AI-generated output with no editorial eye. Ironic given the subject matter; a deliberate tell that must be avoided. Every design decision should be defensible.

## Design Principles

1. **Typography carries the weight.** Bold, considered type choices do more work than decorative elements. The reference (Adam Hartwig's portfolio, minus the yellow) earns its distinctiveness almost entirely through type scale and weight. This is the target register.
2. **Every element is earned.** Nothing decorative, nothing vague. If something can be removed and the page improves, it should be removed. No gradient text, no glassmorphism, no animated blobs by default.
3. **The story is the design.** The belief ladder — return, Meno, process, contact — is a narrative arc. The design should pace that arc, not distract from it.
4. **One color decision.** A single deliberate accent, used sparingly. Not teal-to-cyan (that's the anti-reference). Not yellow (that's Adam's, not Helena's). Something that belongs to this specific person and mission.
5. **Proof through specificity.** Vague claims erode credibility. Every statement should have a specific referent — a named company, a number, a live URL, a named technique. The Meno case study and the Process page are the highest-proof surfaces on the site.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA. All text meets 4.5:1 contrast ratio against its background; large text meets 3:1. Keyboard navigation throughout. Reduced motion alternative for all animations. Appropriate alt text and semantic HTML.
